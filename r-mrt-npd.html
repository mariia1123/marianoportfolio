MRT — Conjoint Analysis for NPD

Group project · RStudio · Autonomous taxi service · March 2023

Group of 3Conjoint analysisLinear regressionRobocab case

Goal

Help Robocab, an autonomous self-driving taxi service, design a winning product by figuring out which service attributes matter most to potential customers — and by how much. The analysis combined LRB Chapter 6 theory on new product development with a hands-on conjoint study in R.

Setup

Five service attributes were tested, each with three levels:

  • Service Provider: City service / Uber / Robocab
  • Car Brand: Google / Toyota / Tesla
  • Waiting time: 0 / 3 / 5 minutes
  • Driving time: 15 / 20 / 25 minutes
  • Price: €10 / €15 / €20

A full factorial would have meant 3⁵ = 243 alternatives, so we used a fractional factorial design showing each respondent 16 random product bundles to rate from 0 to 10. We then ran a rating-based conjoint analysis using OLS regression.

Regression results

The model was highly significant overall (F = 85.52, p < 2.2e-16) with an adjusted R² of 0.176 — modest in absolute terms but reasonable for survey-based behavioural data. Seven of the ten dummy levels came back significant: Provider_Robocab, both Waiting levels, both Driving levels, and both Price levels.

Price drives over half of the decision. Combined with driving time, the two practical attributes account for ~78% of customer preference — branding barely moves the needle.

Price simulation

Predicting preference ratings for a Robocab + Google car + 3-min wait + 15-min drive bundle at three price points:

  • €10: rating 7.73 → ~61% share of preference
  • €15: rating 6.81 → ~58% share
  • €20: rating 5.85 → ~54% share

Recommendations to Robocab

  • Compete on price, not on car brand — customers don't care whether it's a Tesla or a Toyota nearly as much as marketing might suggest
  • Minimise waiting time — going from 5 to 0 minutes is worth nearly half a rating point on its own
  • Reframe attribute level 2 ("3 min wait") to something more realistic like 5–10 minutes for a future study; the original gap was too narrow
  • Add a "style/design" attribute in future research to test whether premium interiors justify a price premium
