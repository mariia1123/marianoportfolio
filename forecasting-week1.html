Forecasting Methods & Performance

Supply Chain Forecasting · BMME075 · February 10, 2026

SoloUK exportsMoving averagesExponential smoothing

Goal

Analyze monthly UK exports to the EU using fundamental forecasting methods. The project involved visualizing trade patterns, computing descriptive statistics, and comparing the forecasting performance of naive, moving average, and Holt's linear exponential smoothing approaches across a 12-month holdout period.

Dataset & Context

Monthly UK exports data spanning January 2018 to February 2026 (94 observations). The dataset captures two major disruptions: the COVID-19 pandemic (2020) which caused a 23.1% decrease in trade with EU countries, and the Brexit transition (2021) which introduced new rules-of-origin requirements and regulatory checks, resulting in 10–15% lower goods exports than pre-Brexit levels.

Methodology

  • Visualization: Identified clear trend breaks and structural shifts coinciding with macroeconomic events
  • Volatility Analysis: Computed running coefficients of variation at 6, 12, and 18-month windows to assess temporal stability
  • Comparative Forecasting: Evaluated naive, MA(3), MA(6), MA(12), and Holt's LES over a 12-month test period
  • Accuracy Metrics: Used ME, RMSE, MAE, MAPE to assess forecast performance

  • MA(12) outperformed all other methods with lowest RMSE (814.4) and MAPE (1.189%), effectively smoothing short-term noise while preserving the true export level
  • Year-over-year changes far more volatile than month-to-month, with 65% higher variation between percentiles
  • Holt's LES performed poorly (RMSE: 5,854.6) due to the absence of significant trend in the differenced series; recent observations were overweighted
  • Structural breaks evident: 2020 pandemic spike and 2021 Brexit transition created forecasting challenges; post-2022 stabilization
The analysis demonstrates that for trend-stationary but high-noise series like trade data, simple moving averages with appropriate window sizes outperform model-based methods that assume global trend components. A 12-month window captures seasonal cycles while filtering out short-term noise.

Insights for Supply Chain

In supply chain forecasting, recognizing the trade-off between trend following and noise reduction is critical. While exponential smoothing adapts to gradual changes, moving averages provide stability when structural patterns are weak. For macroeconomic time series subject to policy shocks (Brexit, tariffs, pandemics), ensemble or robust methods may better capture both systematic and anomalous movements.
