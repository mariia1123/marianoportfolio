Mapping the World of Wine

Solo project · Python · Network analysis of wine reviews · April 2025

SoloNetwork analysisPythonPageRankCentrality

Goal

Explore the global wine industry through network analysis, mapping relationships between wine-producing countries, grape varieties, regions, and critics. Using WineEnthusiast review data, I constructed multiple network visualizations to uncover hidden patterns: which countries dominate production, which varieties are most versatile, and which wines offer exceptional value.

Context

Networks and Simulations course (EBC2184) at Erasmus University Rotterdam, coordinated by Dr. Leto Peel. The project applied network science concepts (PageRank, betweenness centrality, node connectivity) to a real-world wine dataset, revealing how the wine world functions as an interconnected system of producers, varieties, regions, and critics.

Dataset & Methodology

  • Source: WineEnthusiast reviews dataset (from Kaggle, 2017)
  • Scope: First 1,000 rows filtered to European wines only—avoided random sampling bias and geographic skew
  • Cleaning: Removed missing values; focused on wines with complete country, variety, region, price, and rating data
  • Tools: Python with NetworkX, Pandas, and ggplot2 for network construction and visualization

Key Finding: Italy and France are the dominant hubs, connected to many varieties. Red Blend, Riesling, and White Blend show highest versatility, appearing across multiple countries. This network reveals that wine production is highly concentrated among traditional Old World producers.

Network 2: Region-Variety Network

A more granular network mapping 58 European wine regions to 104 varieties. Regions shown in blue, varieties in red. Node size reflects diversity; edge width shows association strength. Filtered to top 20% strongest associations for clarity.
Central Hub:
Sicily & Sardinia (highest PageRank)
Key Connector:
Veneto (highest betweenness centrality)

Key Finding: While Italy dominates at the country level, Sicily & Sardinia emerges as the most central region—connected to many varieties. Veneto acts as a crucial bridge between different parts of the network. Regional specialization is evident: Burgundy links to Pinot Noir, Alsace to Riesling, creating distinct flavor profiles.

Premium-priced wines dominate the network, suggesting most reviewed wines sit in the mid-market range. Nebbiolo bridges expensive and medium quality, hinting at diverse production quality.

Key Insights & Discoveries

  • Geographic concentration: Italy and France control ~31% of the network's centrality, confirming Old World dominance
  • Variety versatility: Red Blend, Riesling, and White Blend appear across regions—they're the "connector" varieties
  • Regional specialization: Each region tends to specialize (Tuscany → Red Blend, Alsace → Riesling), creating distinct flavor profiles
  • Value paradox: The best quality-to-price wine (Cramele Recaș) comes from an underrated region (Romania), suggesting market inefficiencies
  • Critic subjectivity: Tasters show different rating patterns—high-volume critics (Roger Voss: 140 reviews) provide broader market perspective than specialists

Limitations & Challenges

  • Dataset size: 130k total rows reduced to 1,000 (European focus) and further filtered by data quality—small sample bias possible
  • Network density: Over 100 varieties created very dense networks; filtering to top 20% associations improved clarity but lost nuance
  • Critic bias: Tasters with few reviews (n=3-4) vs. many (n=140) have unequal influence; some tasters inflate ratings systematically
  • Subjective "best": Quality-to-price ratios depend on critic preferences—what one taster rates 90 points, another rates 85. Wine appreciation remains deeply personal
  • Temporal snapshot: Data from 2017; current prices and ratings may differ significantly

Conclusion

Network analysis transforms wine review data into actionable insights. Rather than treating wine as isolated products, viewing it as a network reveals:

  • Which countries/regions are hubs vs. periphery
  • Which varieties are versatile vs. specialized
  • Where value lies (often in overlooked regions)
  • How expert opinions differ and influence perception

The analysis confirms traditional wine wisdom (Italy & France dominate) while uncovering non-obvious patterns (Cramele Recaș offers unbeatable value; Sicily is a diversity hub). Yet ultimately, networks and numbers remind us that wine remains a deeply personal experience—the "best" wine is the one you enjoy most.
