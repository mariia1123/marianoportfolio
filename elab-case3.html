Case III — Portfolio Optimization with CAPM

Group project · RStudio · Mean-variance & Security Market Line · May 2023

Group of 4Mean-varianceCAPMCross-validation

Goal

Build optimal investment portfolios using modern portfolio theory and the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM). The analysis involved constructing efficient frontiers, comparing theoretical vs. empirical returns, estimating systematic risk (beta), and validating portfolio performance across in-sample and out-of-sample periods using a rigorous cross-validation methodology.

Data & Setup

We analysed 19 equities from different sectors over 15+ years of daily price history (2003–2023), with particular focus on the 2002–2020 period for robustness. Portfolio-level analysis was conducted using team-assigned stocks, and we supplemented this with analysis of Coca-Cola (KO) to evaluate technical indicators (MACD, RSI) and long-term trends.

Methodology: K-Fold Cross-Validation

We implemented a sophisticated cross-validation approach to avoid overfitting and assess generalization:

  • Data splitting: Split returns into odd and even observations, creating two complementary training and validation folds
  • Fold 1: Train on odd observations, validate on even (out-of-sample)
  • Fold 2: Train on even observations, validate on odd (out-of-sample)
  • Stability testing: Calculated means, variances, and betas separately for each fold to assess parameter consistency
Key finding: Means and variances remained stable across folds, with most stocks clustering near the 45° line in scatter plots—validating the sample structure and risk stability.

Mean-Variance Optimization

We formulated a constrained quadratic program for each fold:

  • Objective: Minimize w' Σ w subject to expected return ≥ target return
  • Constraints: Weights sum to 1; no short-selling allowed (w ≥ 0)
  • Risk aversion parameter: γ = 3, balancing risk and return
  • Solver: Quadratic programming (solve.QP) in R

We solved the model separately for each fold's covariance matrix and mean vector, then tested portfolio weights on out-of-sample data.

Portfolio Performance Results

Odd Fold (Training on Odd, Validating on Even):
In-sample: 8.93% return, 10.1% volatility, Sharpe = 0.788
Out-of-sample: 7.54% return, 11.8% volatility, Sharpe = 0.553
Even Fold (Training on Even, Validating on Odd):
In-sample: 7.75% return, 11.8% volatility, Sharpe = 0.572
Out-of-sample: 8.77% return, 10.1% volatility, Sharpe = 0.773

The decline in Sharpe ratio from in-sample to out-of-sample (~15–30%) is typical for portfolio optimization, reflecting overfitting to historical covariances. Notably, the even-fold portfolio showed stronger out-of-sample performance, suggesting parameter stability.

Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)

We estimated systematic risk (beta) for each stock and derived theoretical returns via the CAPM equation:

  • Beta calculation: Covariance(stock, market proxy) / Variance(market)
  • Risk-free rate: 1% annually
  • Market risk premium: Observed mean excess return of the market proxy
  • CAPM return: r_f + β (r_m - r_f)

We re-optimized portfolios using CAPM-predicted returns instead of historical means, introducing an additional layer of model validation.

Security Market Line (SML)

We visualized the SML by plotting each stock's beta against its sample mean return, superimposing the theoretical CAPM line. Key observations:

  • Stocks above the SML are underpriced (excess return relative to risk)
  • Stocks below the SML are overpriced (insufficient return for risk)
  • Even-fold portfolio had more stocks above its SML, explaining its superior out-of-sample performance

Portfolio Composition

The optimized portfolios were concentrated (top 3 stocks held ~70% weight), with SPY (S&P 500 ETF) allocated approximately 5.75% across both folds. This modest weight reflects:

  • Diversification benefit: Individual stocks offered higher risk-adjusted returns than the broad index in this period
  • Optimization mechanics: The quadratic program chose specific high-return assets over broad market exposure
  • Constraint trade-off: No short-selling restriction meant the optimizer could not hedge overvalued sectors

Technical Analysis: Coca-Cola

As a supplementary analysis, we examined KO stock with technical indicators:

  • MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence): Identified momentum shifts and trend reversals
  • RSI (Relative Strength Index): Detected overbought/oversold conditions
  • Monthly returns: Mean ~1.16%, volatility ~5.91% annualized

Key Takeaways

  • Overfitting is real: In-sample Sharpe ratios (~0.7) consistently outpaced out-of-sample (~0.55–0.77), validating the importance of cross-validation
  • Parameter stability: Betas and means were remarkably consistent across odd/even folds, supporting the theoretical foundations of CAPM
  • SML violations: Systematic arbitrage opportunities (stocks off the SML) existed during this period, though constraints limited exploitation
  • Concentration risk: Unconstrained optimization led to concentrated portfolios; practitioners would add diversification bounds
