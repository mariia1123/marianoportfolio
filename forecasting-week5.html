Ensemble Forecasting & Intermittent Demand

Goal

Forecast Walmart household sales using ensemble methods, then improve the baseline with machine learning on residuals. Tune GBM tree counts, and analyze intermittent demand at the SKU level with Croston's method.

Dataset & Context

Daily household sales from Walmart's M5 Kaggle competition (2011, 2016), 1,845 observations. The series shows a clear upward trend, strong weekly seasonality (likely from weekly shopping routines), and five zero-demand days, all around New Year.

Key Analyses

  • Ensemble: Combined HW, STL, and ARIMA with uniform, OPERA, and hybrid weights
  • ML on residuals: Regression trees, random forest (200 trees), and GBM, using 30 lags, calendar events, and Fourier terms
  • GBM tuning: Tested 50, 750 trees over 30 trials, tracking train vs test error
  • Intermittent demand: All SKUs in HOUSEHOLD_2 at store WI_2, evaluated Croston's via Spearman correlations

  • ARIMA dominates: OPERA assigns ARIMA weights close to one, while HW and STL barely contribute. ARIMA also wins solo (RMSE 786.2 vs HW 856.0, STL 916.4).
  • Ensembles help weak models, not the best one: Combi.own and Combi.opera beat HW and STL, but not ARIMA alone. Ensemble value is about robustness, not always raw accuracy.
  • Random Forest wins on ML: RF+HW reaches the lowest RMSE (782.6), capturing patterns the Holt-Winters baseline misses. Trees actually hurt the baseline (RMSE 926.6), GBM only improves slightly.
  • GBM overfits past the mid-range: Training error drops with more trees, but test error plateaus. Classic case of learning noise rather than useful patterns.
  • Intermittence matters more than lumpiness: Correlation with testMASE is 0.283 vs only 0.029. Croston's struggles with slow-moving items, not with volatile demand sizes.

More trees ≠ better model. Past a point, complexity just captures noise. For Walmart sales, the model would start fitting daily fluctuations instead of broader demand patterns.

Supply Chain Implications

For regular-demand items, ARIMA works well and ML adds modest gains. For slow-moving SKUs, Croston's is necessary, but accuracy drops as intermittence rises. Items with low turnover create the greatest challenges for stock planning, so retailers should segment their forecasting by demand pattern rather than using one method everywhere.
