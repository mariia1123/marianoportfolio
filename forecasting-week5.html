Ensemble Forecasting & Intermittent Demand

Supply Chain Forecasting · BMME075 · March 10, 2026

SoloEnsemble methodsGBM tuningCroston's method

Goal

Forecast Walmart household sales using ensemble methods (HW, STL, ARIMA), enhance baseline forecasts with machine learning (regression trees, random forests, GBM), tune GBM hyperparameters, and analyze intermittent demand using Croston's method and demand characteristics.

Dataset & Context

Daily household product sales (M5 Kaggle competition) spanning 2011–2016 with 1,845 observations. Series exhibits upward trend, strong weekly seasonality, irregular peaks (promotions), and five zero-demand days around New Year. Suitable for demonstrating ensemble, ML enhancement, and intermittent demand techniques.

Key Analyses

  • Ensemble Forecasting: Combined HW, STL, and ARIMA using uniform weights and OPERA dynamic weighting
  • ML Residual Modeling: Predicted HW residuals using regression trees, random forests, and gradient boosting
  • GBM Tuning: Tested 50–750 trees in 50-unit increments; tracked training vs. test error
  • Intermittent Demand: Computed intermittence, lumpiness; assessed Croston's method via Spearman correlations

  • ARIMA dominates ensemble: Receives weights of 0.75–1.0 throughout forecast period, indicating it best captures dynamics; HW and STL contribute minimally
  • Random Forest improves baseline: RF+HW achieves RMSE 782.6 vs. base HW 855.9 (8.5% improvement); regression trees and GBM provide smaller gains
  • GBM overfitting evident beyond 300 trees: Training error continues declining (RMSE/MAE decrease) while test error plateaus/increases; optimal range 200–300 trees balances fit vs. generalization
  • Intermittence more predictive than lumpiness: Spearman correlation: intermittence-MASE = 0.283; lumpiness-MASE = 0.029; Croston's struggles with sparse SKUs
  • Demand characteristics highly correlated: CV², avgPos, sdPos, lambda show correlations >0.94; indicates multicollinearity in demand feature space
Ensemble learning works best when component models are diverse and uncorrelated. Here, ARIMA's dominance suggests HW/STL are redundant. For retail forecasting, ensemble value lies in robustness rather than accuracy—averaging hedge against model misspecification.

Supply Chain Implications

Ensemble and ML methods improve accuracy but require computational overhead. For slow-moving items (high intermittence), Croston's method outperforms classical forecasts; traditional methods suit regular demand. Tuning involves trade-off: too many trees overfit; too few underfit. Cross-validation guides practical selection.
