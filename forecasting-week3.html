ARIMA, Non-Stationarity & Changing Volatility

Supply Chain Forecasting, BMME075, February 24, 2026

SoloARIMAADF / KPSSGARCH
View PDF
Bottom line

Weekly heating oil returns are best modeled with ARIMA(0,1,1) for the mean and GARCH(1,1) with Student-t errors for the variance. Refit weekly, this combo cuts holdout RMSE to 0.028.

Context

Heating oil is a volatile commodity, responsive to geopolitics, weather, and the broader oil complex. The series shows both trending levels and clustering volatility in returns. Modeling it well means handling two things at once: mean dynamics (ARIMA) and changing variance (GARCH).

The diagnostic path

01

Levels won't sit still

Log prices drift across crises (2008, 2020, 2021-23). Both tests confirm non-stationarity, ADF -2.65KPSS 5.91. Differenced, however, the series fluctuates around zero and both flip, ADF -25.7KPSS 0.08. One difference is enough.

02

The differenced series reads as MA(1)

ACF and PACF both spike at lag 1, but neither decays gradually. Since AR(1) would need that decay, the cleaner read is MA(1) on the differences, meaning ARIMA(0,1,1) on the levels.

03

ARIMA(0,1,1) wins the model contest

Across six specifications, ARIMA(0,1,1) has the lowest BIC at -4530.9, and Ljung-Box is clean at lags 12, 24 and 30. ARIMA(2,1,0) beats it on AIC by only 0.6, not enough to justify the extra parameter.

04

The mean is clean, the variance is not

Even so, the squared residuals from an auxiliary ARMA(3,2) still show structure, ACF tails off, PACF cuts after lag 3. That is the classic ARCH signature, so the mean is captured but the variance is not.

05

Volatility clusters, and tails are fat

Four variance specs were fit on the same ARMA(3,2) mean. GARCH(1,1) with Student-t errors wins, log-likelihood 2464.7 and a significant shape parameter. α + β close to 1 means shocks decay slowly, typical of energy markets around shocks.

Conditional volatility, GARCH(1,1)-t
A calm baseline most of the time, with sharp clusters around shocks. That envelope is what GARCH-t is fitting.
06

Refitting weekly is what actually helps

Fixed-window forecasts barely separate ARIMA from GARCH, both miss short-term moves. Refit one step at a time, however, rolling GARCH lands at RMSE 0.028MAE 0.023, versus ARIMA rolling at 0.034 / 0.027 and fixed-window at 0.035 / 0.028.

Supply Chain Note

Point forecasts alone aren't enough. Since volatility is itself predictable and clusters around shocks, planning should carry a time-varying uncertainty band, not a fixed one. Procurement, hedging, and safety stock read very differently when the next week looks calm versus stressed. GARCH-t provides that band, refit weekly to keep it honest.
